\theabstract{In private cloud infrastructures, service outages caused by resource exhaustion are often reflected in log and metric data long before threshold-based alarm systems raise alerts. This thesis presents CloudMon, an end-to-end AIOps system designed to support proactive capacity management in OpenStack-based private cloud environments.

CloudMon comprises four main components. In the first component, raw metrics collected from Nova, Neutron, and Keystone services are combined with system logs in a shared feature space, yielding 272-dimensional hybrid feature vectors. In the second component, 42 capacity forecasting models spanning seven algorithms are trained for vCPU, memory, and disk resources across 24-hour and 72-hour forecasting horizons. In the third component, a four-stage log processing pipeline covers trend detection, z-score anomaly detection, pre-failure pattern matching, and cascade risk identification. In the final component, SHAP explainability analysis and a large language model (LLM)-based recommendation engine are combined to deliver justified, actionable suggestions to cloud operators.

Experiments conducted on a real OpenStack cluster over the April--June 2026 period reveal that Linear Regression substantially outperforms all tree-based algorithms at the 24-hour horizon, achieving R\textsuperscript{2}\,=\,0.9577 for vCPU and R\textsuperscript{2}\,=\,0.7945 for memory. SHAP analysis demonstrates that log-derived features occupy four of the top ten positions in the vCPU forecasting model, quantitatively confirming that log--metric correlation contributes meaningfully to capacity prediction. The log pipeline detected that \textit{slow\_response} log counts increase by 287\% on average 68 hours before a failure event, and identified a cascade risk arising from the simultaneous degradation of three services. The LLM layer synthesized capacity status, SHAP explanations, and log anomalies into a single operational report containing 9 prioritized recommendations---2 critical and 7 warnings.}

\keywords{AIOps, Capacity Forecasting, Explainable Artificial Intelligence, Log Analysis, Large Language Model, OpenStack.}
